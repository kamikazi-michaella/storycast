Lina and the Last Bus

Written story · about 3 minutes · An original retelling of the Cinderella fairy tale

The night shift

Lina worked every evening at her aunt's tailoring shop, long after her cousins had gone out. While they laughed on the street, she hemmed trousers, stitched buttons and swept the floor. Nobody thanked her. Nobody asked what she wanted. But every night, on the walk home, she hummed a tune she had made up herself.

The invitation

One Friday, a poster appeared in the shop window: a city music night, open to anyone who could sing. Lina read it three times. Her cousins laughed. “You? On a stage?” her aunt said, and handed her a bag of mending that would take all night.

Lina finished the mending by sunset, faster than she ever had. Then she folded the last shirt, picked up her bag and ran.

The last bus

The bus was almost empty. An older woman sat near the back, knitting. She looked at Lina’s worn shoes and her ironed dress, and she smiled. “You look like someone going somewhere important,” she said. Lina shrugged. “I just want to sing one song.” The woman nodded. “Then sing it well.” She pressed a small silver pin into Lina’s hand “for luck.”

The stage

The hall was loud and bright. When her name was called, Lina’s knees shook. She fastened the pin to her collar, closed her eyes and hummed the first notes of her own tune. Then she sang. The room grew quiet. By the final line, people were standing.

The pin

The clock struck eleven and Lina ran for the door, remembering the last bus. In her hurry, the silver pin fell and rolled across the floor. A young organiser picked it up, turned it in the light and looked for her, but she was gone.

The next morning, the organiser walked the whole neighbourhood, asking at every shop, until he came to a small tailoring shop with a light still on. Inside, a girl was sweeping. She looked up, and he held out the pin. “I think this belongs to the girl who sang,” he said.

Lina took it and smiled. This time, she did not run.
